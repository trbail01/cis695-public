# Team-Building Activity: Smart Home Challenge Cards

This activity gives students a structured, low-pressure way to meet one another before the technical labs begin.

It is designed for grades 8-12, works well with mixed experience levels, and reinforces the same problem-solving mindset used later in the camp.

---

## Purpose

- Help students learn names quickly
- Reduce awkwardness before group lab work
- Encourage listening, collaboration, and creativity
- Introduce smart-home problem-solving in a fun, non-technical format

---

## Recommended Timing

- **When to use:** After the welcome and before Lab 1
- **Length:** 15 to 20 minutes
- **Group size:** 3 to 4 students per team

---

## Materials

- One printed challenge card per group
- Scratch paper or small whiteboard
- Markers or pens
- Optional name tags

---

## Setup

Prepare 4 to 6 short prompt cards. Each card should describe a simple real-world problem that a smart-home system could help solve.

Example prompts:

- A grandparent wants a light to turn on automatically at night when someone walks into the hallway.
- A family wants to know when a back door opens after school.
- A student wants a bedroom lamp to turn off automatically at bedtime.
- A teacher wants a room status light to turn on when a door opens for visitors.
- A family wants a reminder when a space has been empty for a while and lights are still on.

---

## Student Instructions

1. Form a group of 3 to 4 students.
2. Share names, grade level, and one technology or app you use often.
3. Pick a challenge card.
4. As a team, decide:
   - What problem is happening?
   - What sensor could detect it?
   - What smart device could respond?
   - What should happen in one simple "if this, then that" sentence?
5. Write the idea in plain language.
6. Share the idea with the room in a 30 to 60 second explanation.

---

## Facilitator Notes

- Keep the tone playful and low stakes.
- If students are shy, let them appoint one spokesperson while everyone contributes ideas.
- If a group gets stuck, prompt them with simple questions about what should be detected and what should happen next.
- Reinforce that there is no single correct answer as long as the team can explain the logic.
- Use this activity to preview the vocabulary of sensor, controller, smart device, trigger, and action.

---

## Why This Works

This activity supports the camp because it:

- Starts relationship building without using a generic icebreaker
- Keeps the theme connected to IoT from the first few minutes
- Gives students an early success before they touch the hardware
- Helps instructors see which students are quiet, confident, collaborative, or may need support later

---

## Transition to Lab 1

Close the activity by pointing out that each team just described the same logic they will build during camp:

- something is detected
- the system makes a decision
- a device responds

That short bridge makes the team-building activity feel like part of the learning experience rather than a separate add-on.
